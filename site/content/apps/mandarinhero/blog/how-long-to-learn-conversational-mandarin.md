The honest answer: **fluent Mandarin takes years, but real everyday conversations can come within a few months.** How fast depends mostly on your goal and how regularly you practise.

## What the numbers say

The US Foreign Service Institute (FSI), which trains American diplomats, puts Mandarin in its hardest category. It estimates **88 weeks of full-time classes, about 2,200 class hours**, to reach professional working proficiency. For comparison, Spanish takes 600 to 750 hours.

That sounds daunting, but it's the bar for diplomats who negotiate trade policy in Chinese. If you want to order food, find your way around and chat with friends, the bar is much lower.

## Realistic milestones (at 15–20 minutes a day)

| After about | You can |
|---|---|
| 2–4 weeks | Introduce yourself, count, ask prices, say "I'll have this one" and "thanks" |
| 2–3 months | Order food, take a taxi, ask the way, have simple chats |
| 6 months | Get through everyday situations and follow the answers, if people don't speak too fast |
| 1–2 years | Hold real conversations about your life, work and plans |
| several years | Speak fluently, on complex topics too |

This timeline is for **speaking and listening**. Reading and writing characters takes extra time. That's why many learners start by speaking and add reading at their own pace.

## What makes Mandarin hard, and what doesn't

**Hard:**

- **Tones.** mā, má, mǎ and mà are four different words, and your ear needs time to tune in.
- **Characters.** Reading means learning hundreds, eventually thousands, of them.
- **Few familiar words.** Unlike French or Spanish, almost nothing looks like English.

**Easier than you'd think:**

- **No conjugation.** 我去, 你去, 他去: "go" is always 去.
- **No articles, plurals, gender or cases.**
- **Logical words.** 电脑 (diànnǎo) is literally "electric brain": a computer.

## How to get there faster

1. **Learn phrases, not lone words.** 我要这个 (*wǒ yào zhège*, "I'll have this one") gets you further than ten isolated nouns.
2. **Say everything out loud.** Speaking is a skill your mouth needs to practise, not just your head.
3. **Practise a little every day.** Spaced repetition brings each word back just before you'd forget it, so it sticks for the least effort.
4. **Learn the language people really speak.** Learn phrases for real situations like ordering food or asking the way, not textbook dialogues. Natural phrasing, like the particles in [啊, 呢, 吧 and 嘛](/apps/mandarinhero/blog/chinese-particles-a-ne-ba-ma/), makes you understood faster.
