You learn a word on Monday, and by Friday it's gone. That's not a problem with your memory. It's how memory works. **Spaced repetition** is the most effective fix we know of.

## The forgetting curve

In the 1880s, psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus measured how quickly we forget new information. His **forgetting curve** drops steeply. Most of what you learn fades within days unless you revisit it.

But each time you recall something, it fades more slowly. Review a word after a day, then after three days, then a week, and it's soon stuck for weeks or months.

## How spaced repetition works

1. You learn a word.
2. It comes back the next day. If you remember it, the gap grows.
3. A few days later, then a week, then weeks.
4. If you forget, it comes back sooner.

Each review happens **just before you'd forget**, when it's hard enough to strengthen the memory but not so late that you have to relearn it. Easy words fade into the background, and your time goes to the words you struggle with.

## Using it well for Chinese

**Learn phrases, not lone words.** 打包 (*dǎbāo*, "to pack up food") is easier to keep, and far more useful, as **这个帮我打包一下** ("Could you pack this up for me?"). The sentence gives the word context, grammar and a moment to use it.

**Say it out loud.** If you only read your cards, you'll recognise words but not produce them. Say every answer aloud, with tones. (See [the tones guide](/apps/mandarinhero/blog/mandarin-tones-guide/).)

**Hear it.** Every card should have audio. Your ear needs as much training as your memory.

**Keep new words modest.** Five to ten new words or phrases a day adds up to more than two thousand a year. If reviews pile up, cut back on new words, not on reviews.

**Do it every day.** Ten minutes daily beats an hour on Sunday. The schedule only works if you show up when the words are due.
