In China, you rarely see cash change hands. You **scan**. Here's the Mandarin for paying anywhere, from the market to a group dinner, with lines from [Mandarin Hero](/apps/mandarinhero/)'s lessons.

## Scanning: who scans whom?

Payments run through **支付宝** (*Zhīfùbǎo*, Alipay) and **微信支付** (*Wēixìn Zhīfù*, WeChat Pay). There are two ways to pay:

- **You scan them:** the shop shows its **收款码** (*shōukuǎnmǎ*, payment QR code), you scan it and type the amount.
- **They scan you:** you open your **付款码** (*fùkuǎnmǎ*, pay code) and the cashier scans it.

So the question you'll ask most is:

> 我扫你还是你扫我？ — Do I scan you, or do you scan me?
>
> 我扫你，打开付款码。 — I'll scan you. Open your payment code.

And the phrases you'll hear most are **扫这个码就行** ("just scan this code") and **收到了** ("got it", once the payment comes through).

## Saying prices

In speech, a yuan is **块** (*kuài*). On price tags it's **元** (*yuán*). A tenth is **毛** (*máo*).

- **五十六块八** — 56.80
- **三毛** — 0.30
- **二十五块一斤** — 25 a *jin* (half a kilo, the standard unit at markets)

Useful questions:

- **这个多少钱？** *Zhège duōshǎo qián?* — How much is this?
- **一共多少钱？** *Yígòng duōshǎo qián?* — How much altogether?
- **可以用现金吗？** *Kěyǐ yòng xiànjīn ma?* — Can I pay cash?

## Haggling (at markets, not in shops)

> 老板，这个多少钱？ — How much is this?
>
> 一百五。 — 150.
>
> 太贵了，五十吧。 — Too much. How about 50?
>
> 五十不行，最低一百。 — Not 50. A hundred is the lowest.
>
> 那我再看看。 — Then I'll keep looking.
>
> 哎，回来回来，八十给你！ — Hey, come back, come back! Eighty, it's yours!

**那我再看看** ("then I'll keep looking") is the most powerful haggling line there is. Haggling is normal at markets and with street sellers, but not in supermarkets, chain stores or restaurants.

## Splitting the bill with friends

- **我们AA吧。** *Wǒmen AA ba.* — Let's split it.
- **一人一百二。** *Yì rén yìbǎi èr.* — 120 each.
- **我转你。** *Wǒ zhuǎn nǐ.* — I'll transfer it to you.
- **收到，我转你了。** *Shōudào, wǒ zhuǎn nǐ le.* — Got it, I've sent you the money.

## Red packets: 红包

**红包** (*hóngbāo*) were once red envelopes with cash. Now they're mostly digital, sent in WeChat chats. In group chats, someone sends one, and everyone races to **抢** (*qiǎng*, grab) a random share:

> 我发个红包，大家抢！ — I'm sending a red packet. Grab it, everyone!
>
> 抢到了！两块八，哈哈。 — Got it! 2.80, haha.
>
> 手气不错！ — Lucky you!

Red packets are everywhere at Chinese New Year, from parents, bosses and group chats.

## Tipping

Tipping isn't part of the culture in mainland China. If you're unsure, ask: **要给小费吗？** (*yào gěi xiǎofèi ma?*, "Should I tip?"). The answer is almost always **不用** ("no need").
