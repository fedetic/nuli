But it fits best once you know the basics:** you can read pinyin, you hear the tones, you know a few hundred words, and maybe you've finished a beginner course. That's the point where learning Chinese gets hardest, and where most apps have the least to offer.

Here's why that stage is so hard, and which kind of app fits which kind of learner.

## Why the step after the basics is the hardest

### 1. The vocabulary explodes

The basics are small. In the HSK 2.0 word lists, **HSK 1 is 150 words and HSK 2 is 300 in total**. Then it grows fast: **600 words for HSK 3, 1,200 for HSK 4, 2,500 for HSK 5 and 5,000 for HSK 6.**

So the first two levels together are about 6% of the full list. Most of the work comes after. And for an English or Dutch speaker almost no Chinese word sounds familiar, so every one of them has to be learned, kept and found again in a conversation.

That's a memory problem first. Reading a word once doesn't make it stick. Being asked for it again just before you'd forget does. We wrote about the research behind that in [guess, struggle, remember](/apps/mandarinhero/blog/best-way-to-memorize-vocabulary/).

### 2. Real conversations don't sound like your course

Beginner courses use clear, careful sentences. Then you talk to a real person. Here's how a chat with a friend goes in Mandarin Hero's *Chatting with a friend* lesson:

> **在干嘛？** Zài gànmá? — What are you up to?
> **上班摸鱼中，哈哈。你呢？** Shàngbān mōyú zhōng, hāhā. Nǐ ne? — Slacking off at work, haha. You?
> **我今天又要加班，无语。** Wǒ jīntiān yòu yào jiābān, wúyǔ. — Overtime again today. Ugh.
> **对啊，太卷了。我想躺平。** Duì a, tài juǎn le. Wǒ xiǎng tǎngpíng. — Yep, it's such a rat race. I want to lie flat.

None of it is hard, but hardly any of it is in a beginner course: **干嘛** instead of 做什么, **摸鱼** for slacking off, **无语** for "ugh", **卷** and **躺平**. And little words like **呢** and **啊** carry half the meaning. We wrote about those in [啊, 呢, 吧, 嘛](/apps/mandarinhero/blog/chinese-particles-a-ne-ba-ma/).

### 3. Knowing words isn't the same as using them

Many learners hit this wall: they know hundreds of words on flashcards and still freeze when someone talks to them. Recognising a word with time to think is one skill. Catching it in fast speech and answering right away is another. We looked at this in [is Anki good for learning Chinese?](/apps/mandarinhero/blog/is-anki-good-for-learning-chinese/), with real learners' stories.

### 4. Progress feels slower

In the first weeks every lesson is a big step. After the basics, a hundred new words barely feels like progress, even though it is. That's when a lot of people quit, or go back to the beginner app they've already finished.

## What helps at this stage

The fix is the same for all four: **lots of words, kept for good, used in real conversations, out loud.** That's what we built [Mandarin Hero](/apps/mandarinhero/) around.

- **Lessons for real situations.** 32 of them, from ordering food and taking the metro to the hospital and a WeChat group chat. Each one teaches its key words, then its handy phrases, then the conversation itself, with audio on every line.
- **Skip what you know.** A word you already know gets one tap, **I know this already**, so you spend your time on what's new. That matters when you're not starting from zero.
- **Play your part out loud.** In the role-play you say your lines, with natural alternatives for each one. Missed what they said? Ask **不好意思，再说一遍？** (Bù hǎoyìsi, zài shuō yí biàn?, "Sorry, say that again?") and they say it another way.
- **Flashcards that keep it.** Every word and phrase from a lesson comes back right before you'd forget it. A word only counts as mastered once every card for it is at a 21-day interval.
- **All of HSK 1 to 6 when you want more.** Switch on a level in Flashcards, with Listen, Speak, Read and Write cards, so the vocabulary grows next to the conversations.
