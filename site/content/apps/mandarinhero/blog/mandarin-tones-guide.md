Tones scare most beginners, but they're more manageable than they look. Here's what they sound like, the shortcuts natives take, and how to practise without overthinking.

## The four tones (plus one)

| Tone | Mark | How it sounds | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | ā | high and flat, like holding a note | 妈 mā (mum) |
| 2nd | á | rising, like a surprised "Huh?" | 麻 má (hemp; numb) |
| 3rd | ǎ | low, dipping down | 马 mǎ (horse) |
| 4th | à | falling sharply, like a firm "No!" | 骂 mà (to scold) |
| neutral | a | short and light | 吗 ma (question word) |

So **妈妈骂马吗？** (*māma mà mǎ ma?*) means "Is mum scolding the horse?". It's the classic tongue twister for practising all of them.

## The 3rd tone is mostly low

Textbooks draw the 3rd tone as a dip and a rise. In normal speech, the rise usually disappears: it's just **low**. Only at the end of a sentence, or when it's said slowly, do you hear the full dip and rise. Think "low" and you'll sound more natural straight away.

## Tone changes natives make

Some tones change depending on the next syllable. You'll hear these in almost every sentence.

**Two 3rd tones in a row:** the first becomes a 2nd.

- 你好 is written *nǐ hǎo* but said **ní hǎo**
- 很好 *hěn hǎo* → **hén hǎo**

**不 (bù, "not")** becomes 2nd tone before a 4th tone:

- 不是 → **bú shì** (not 'bù shì')
- 不要 → **bú yào**
- 不客气 → **bú kèqi**

**一 (yī, "one")** changes too:

- before a 4th tone it becomes 2nd: 一个 → **yí ge**, 一下 → **yíxià**
- before 1st, 2nd and 3rd tones it becomes 4th: 一起 → **yìqǐ**, 一点 → **yìdiǎn**
- counting or at the end it stays **yī**: 第一 *dì yī*

## The neutral tone

Many common words end in a short, light syllable without a tone: **谢谢** *xièxie*, **朋友** *péngyou*, **东西** *dōngxi*. The particles 吗, 呢, 吧 and 啊 are all neutral. (More on those in [啊, 呢, 吧 and 嘛 explained](/apps/mandarinhero/blog/chinese-particles-a-ne-ba-ma/).)

## How to practise tones

1. **Learn every word with its tone, from day one.** Fixing it later is much harder.
2. **Learn tones in phrases, not syllables.** 谢谢 and 不客气 each have their own melody. Remember the melody.
3. **Listen first, then say it out loud.** Copy the audio, exaggerating at first. Your mouth needs the practice, not just your ears.
4. **Use your hand.** Draw the tone in the air as you speak: flat, up, down low, sharp down. It sounds silly, and it works.
5. **Don't freeze.** In a real conversation, context carries you. Speak, get understood, and keep improving.
