Chinese New Year (**春节**, *Chūnjié*, the Spring Festival) is the biggest celebration of the year. In 2027 it falls on **6 February**, starting the **Year of the Goat** (羊年, *yáng nián*). Here are the greetings you'll really hear, and what to say back.

## The three everyone uses

- **新年快乐！** xīnnián kuàilè! — Happy New Year!
- **过年好！** guònián hǎo! — Happy New Year! (very common in speech)
- **恭喜发财！** gōngxǐ fācái! — Wishing you prosperity! (literally: congratulations, get rich)

The cheeky reply to 恭喜发财, especially from kids and friends, is **红包拿来！** (*hóngbāo ná lái!*): "Hand over the red packet!"

## Wishes for family, colleagues and friends

| Wish | Pinyin | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 身体健康 | shēntǐ jiànkāng | good health |
| 万事如意 | wànshì rúyì | may everything go as you wish |
| 心想事成 | xīn xiǎng shì chéng | may your wishes come true |
| 工作顺利 | gōngzuò shùnlì | may work go smoothly |
| 羊年大吉 | yáng nián dàjí | good luck in the Year of the Goat |

Combine them with **祝你…** (*zhù nǐ*, "I wish you…"): **祝你身体健康，万事如意！** means "Wishing you good health and all the best!"

## Why is 福 upside down?

On doors you'll see the character **福** (*fú*, good fortune), often hung upside down. This exchange is from Mandarin Hero's "Post & greetings" lesson:

> 这个福字送你，倒着贴在门上。 — This 福 is for you. Stick it on your door upside down.
>
> 为什么要倒着贴？ — Why upside down?
>
> "福倒了"，听起来就是"福到了"嘛！ — "Fortune's upside down" sounds just like "fortune has arrived"!
>
> 哈哈，原来是这样！ — Ha, so that's why!

**倒** (*dào*, upside down) and **到** (*dào*, to arrive) sound the same. It's a pun you'll see on every door.

## Red packets: 红包

At New Year, children and unmarried young people get **红包** (*hóngbāo*): red envelopes with money. These days they also fly around WeChat groups, where everyone races to **抢** (*qiǎng*, grab) a random share. More on that in [paying in China](/apps/mandarinhero/blog/paying-in-china-mandarin-phrases/).

## More New Year words

- **年夜饭** niányèfàn — the big family dinner on New Year's Eve
- **拜年** bàinián — visiting family and friends to wish them a happy new year
- **春晚** Chūnwǎn — the New Year's Eve TV gala that half of China watches
- **放鞭炮** fàng biānpào — setting off firecrackers
